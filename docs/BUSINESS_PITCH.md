# Peerdrop — Business value and pitch

## The pitch

Your first weeks at Kellogg are full of introductions. You remember that someone worked in healthcare and loves horror movies—but not their name, their number, or where you met.

Peerdrop turns that introduction into a connection you can pick up again. Exchange a personal card with consent, preserve a memory, and find people later through fragments or your social graph. Shared classes, clubs, and upcoming events give you a reason to reconnect.

We start with new MBA students, where meeting many people quickly creates a concentrated memory problem. Peerdrop is free for students; our initial business hypothesis is that MBA programs could license it for incoming cohorts to support connection and belonging. An orientation pilot would test whether students use it repeatedly and whether program teams value the outcome enough to pay.

**Remember the person. Restart the conversation.**

## Value by stakeholder

| Stakeholder | Problem | Proposed value | Evidence to seek |
|---|---|---|---|
| New students | Names, personal details, and contact information get lost | Easier recall and a concrete path to follow up | Can students find someone from fragments and reconnect? |
| MBA/student-experience teams | Helping a new cohort form meaningful connections | A student-facing tool supporting orientation and ongoing belonging | Does it improve reported connection, and is there a budget owner? |
| Clubs/event organizers | Introductions may end when an event ends | Context and links that help attendees continue relationships | Do people return to event-linked connections and activities? |

## Who pays?

Recommended first hypothesis: an MBA program or student-experience team buys a cohort or annual license; students use the core product free. This is a recommendation to validate, not a confirmed buyer or pricing model.

A future institutional package could include cohort onboarding, catalog setup, event links, support, and aggregate adoption reporting. These are commercial extensions, not hackathon requirements. A school license does not grant access to private notes, relationship status, individual contact details, or students’ personal social graphs.

Club/event packages are a secondary route. A student subscription is deferred until recurring personal value is demonstrated. Do not introduce ads or sell personal relationship data into the initial model.

Do not invent a price, revenue forecast, market size, or ROI for the pitch. First identify the budget owner, purchasing process, perceived value, and comparable spending through interviews.

## Why this concept is distinctive

The proposed differentiation is the combination of consented exchange, personal meeting memory, and a searchable graph of people already met. The graph supports recall and shared context; it is not a public popularity scoreboard.

The product must be useful after one exchange, before a large network exists. Event links and common classes/clubs offer reasons to return. Live integrations are future possibilities, not partnerships or capabilities we currently have.

## Pilot and validation

Start with one willing orientation group or club event. Have participants complete real exchanges and later try to find someone using incomplete memories. Ask whether they followed up, what they would otherwise have used, and whether capturing a memory interrupted the conversation.

Track exchange completion, successful recall, repeat use, and self-reported follow-up. Agree on pilot success thresholds with the organizer before launch; there is no validated baseline yet. Collect only necessary measurement data and exclude note contents and sensitive profile fields.

Interview student-experience staff about ownership and budget separately from testing student usability. Strong student feedback does not by itself establish institutional willingness to pay.

## Main assumptions to test

- Students will initiate “Want to Peerdrop?” and tolerate a brief reciprocal exchange.
- Shared details and short reminders are enough to improve recall.
- The graph helps users understand their relationships rather than distracting them.
- Students return beyond orientation, helped by shared classes, clubs, and events.
- A school values these outcomes and has an appropriate purchasing route.

## Suggested closing for the demo

“We’re not measuring success by how many names you collect. We want to help you find that person again—and have a reason to continue the conversation. Our next step is an orientation pilot to test recall, follow-through, and repeat use.”
